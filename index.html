Literacy has improved, but learning’s foundation remains uneven

Brandi Robinson

I wanted this project to focus on learning. Literacy is a starting point because reading and writing help people access information and keep learning. But a literacy rate alone does not tell us how good someone’s education is.

This interactive Tableau dashboard looks at two questions: How has adult literacy changed since 2000, and where do the available data still show gaps?

Open the interactive literacy dashboard.

What the data show

Worldwide, adult literacy went from 81.12% in 2000 to 87.74% in 2024. South Asia went from 59.52% to 78.23%, an increase of 18.71 percentage points. Sub-Saharan Africa also improved, from 56.57% to 68.69%, but was still below the worldwide rate. There is progress in all three, but the gaps have not gone away.

The country chart gives a closer look. Chad’s latest available rate is 30.63% from 2019, while Niger’s is 35.61% from 2022. Those dates matter. The chart shows the latest available records, not a comparison where every country was measured in the same year.

What changed and why

The starting point is MakeoverMonday’s 2024 Week 39 literacy project, which links to Our World in Data’s original literacy visualization.

The original lets readers explore literacy across places and over time. This dashboard gives the reader a more focused comparison. The line chart puts the world, South Asia, and Sub-Saharan Africa together from 2000 through 2024, so it is easier to follow their progress and see the gaps. The bar chart shows the latest country records from 2015 onward, ordered from lowest to highest. All bars start at zero, and regional totals are kept separate from countries.

The charts sit side by side, with the main finding and instructions above them. The country bars have values, and the labels include the year. That gives readers the numbers and timing without having to guess. The notes also explain what literacy measures, so the chart is not mistaken for a measure of overall learning quality.

What to keep in mind

How the data were collected: These rates cover basic reading and writing for people aged 15 and older. Countries use different census and survey methods, sometimes relying on people to report their own literacy. They also do not all report in the same years, which limits how closely the figures can be compared.

What was included: The country chart includes 123 countries and territories with a record from 2015 onward. It uses the latest available year for each. Countries without a recent record are left out, not treated as having zero literacy. Missing values were not filled in. This keeps older records out of the comparison, but it also means the chart does not represent every country.

What the results mean: Regional averages can hide large differences between countries. The charts show where rates changed, but not what caused those changes. They also do not measure how well people use reading skills in more complex situations, school quality, or individual learning outcomes.

Where the data came from

The data come from UNESCO UIS through Our World in Data. The original CSV in the MakeoverMonday archive is unavailable. This project therefore uses the updated source CSV, downloaded in September 2026. The source lists a May 2026 update. It is important to note that this is updated data, not the original 2024 file.
